Identifying refugees and IDPs in household surveys: where the revised EGRISS questions stand, and how they compare with your modules

Your modules follow the questions EGRISS published in 2023. We have since checked how those questions work in real surveys, rewritten them and tested the new wording with respondents. Here is what we learned, the new questions, and how they compare with yours.

1 · Context

Why the questions are being revised

The 2023 EGRISS methodological paper proposed a set of questions to identify refugees and IDPs in household surveys, in line with the International Recommendations on Refugee Statistics (IRRS) and on IDP Statistics (IRIS). That first version was published without prior testing, and its use in recent surveys showed gaps. The aim of the second edition is a tested, comparable global standard.

EGRISS High-Level Steering Committee · March 2026

Named a strategic priority

At its annual meeting in New York, the Steering Committee singled out standardised identification questions for refugees, IDPs and stateless persons as a strategic priority for EGRISS’s fourth mandate. The World Bank committed to promote the resulting guidance through its survey operations, and STATAFRIC named the standardisation of tools, so that data can be compared and aggregated, among the challenges for the continent.

How surveys ask the three hardest questions

The desk review found the most variation, and the most errors, in three places: whether someone fled and why, whether they crossed a border, and whether they applied for protection. For each, the 2023 EGRISS wording (which your modules follow), the revised wording, and what surveys actually ask.

2023 paper · your RF3A–RF4, ID1–ID2

“While living in [country], have you ever had to flee home?” Yes or no, then “What is the main reason why you had to flee home?” One of six codes; eviction and personal reasons end the module.

What this shows. Conflict, persecution and disasters appear almost everywhere, but are grouped differently each time, so the same person can be classified differently depending on the survey. Eviction is counted as forced displacement in some surveys and ends the module in others, yours included. Nearly all surveys accept one main reason only; the revised module and the Honduras survey accept every reason that applies.

2023 paper · your RF5, ID4

“After you were forced to leave home, did you cross an international border?”

→
Revised · only if the home fled was in [survey country]

“After you fled your home, did you ever move to another country even if this was only temporary?” People who fled a home in another country are not asked: it is clear they left it.

What this shows. Four questionnaires, yours included, still say “cross an international border”, which respondents can read as passing a checkpoint. Most others already say “move” or “go to another country”, and Mali’s EMOP names the neighbouring countries as examples. None adds “even if this was only temporary”, so short stays abroad, which separate IDPs from returning refugees, go unrecorded.

2023 paper · your RF7

“While in [other country] / [survey country], did you apply for international protection?”

→
After the desk review

“Did you ever apply for international protection, such as refugee status?” with local examples: “did you go to an office like … to register for …”

→
After testing in Thailand · latest

“While living abroad did you ever apply for refugee status?” No respondent understood “international protection”; all understood “refugee status”.

What this shows. Surveys use four different framings: international protection, asylum or refugee status, registration with the government or UNHCR, or simply “papers to stay”. Registration wording works where refugees are recognised as a group on arrival, as in Thailand, but misses people who applied and were refused. This is why the revision asks about refugee status in plain words and lets countries add local examples.

4 · The revised questionnaire

The revised EGRISS questions

Beyond the second edition

Feeding into the revised IRRS

EGRISS is preparing the revision of its International Recommendations. The tested identification questions from this methodological work will feed into the revised International Recommendations on Refugee Statistics (IRRS), so modules aligned now stay aligned with the next version of the standard.
